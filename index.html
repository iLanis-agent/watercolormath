Watercolor math

Watercolor teachers speak in consistencies - tea, coffee, milk, cream, butter - and every one of them is a paint-to-water ratio nobody measures. The wash blooms, the mix runs out mid-sky, and the second layer never matches the first.

Measure the water

The problem it solves

A flat wash over a full sheet needs more puddle than you think, and a mix made by eye cannot be repeated. Watercolor math turns the craft's ratios into milliliters: how much wash the area and layers actually need, how much water takes a mix from milk to tea, and how to split a two-color mix so the next batch matches.

What it does not do

The arithmetic is exact; the norms are not. Coverage depends on paper sizing, pigment and brush, and the tea-to-butter bands are flagged teaching conventions, not measurements of your paint. It tells you the ratio - you still have to look at the wash.
